# Mango Crop Management — India (Telangana and Andhra Pradesh)

## Scope

This source file covers **mango (*Mangifera indica* L.) crop management** for farmers in India, with practical relevance to Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. It focuses on orchard establishment and routine management, **integrated pest management (IPM), disease management, nutritional disorders/deficiencies, fertilizer use, organic/biological options, chemical pesticide options, harvest and pre-harvest cautions**, and official extension/verification routes. It does not make medical claims and does not cover a government scheme.

The principal technical source is the Government of India Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage (DPPQS/PPQS) **IPM Package of Practices for Mango (September 2022)**, compiled with review from ICAR–Central Institute of Sub Tropical Horticulture. The National Horticulture Board (NHB) mango production-technology page and ICAR's 2025 Kharif Agro-Advisory supplement it. Andhra Pradesh's Department of Horticulture portal is used only for the current official state contact/portal context.

## Structured crop-management entries

### 1. Baseline establishment and orchard management

- **Soil & Climate:** Deep, well-drained loamy/alluvial soil rich in organic matter (pH 5.5–7.5).
- **Planting Season:** July–August (rainfed) or February–March (irrigated).
- **Spacing:** 10 m × 10 m (traditional) or 5 m × 5 m / 2.5 m × 2.5 m (high-density varieties like Amrapali/Sindhu).
- **Pruning:** Train young plants. Prune crisscross/dried branches in December or June–July after harvest followed by 1% Bordeaux mixture spray.
- **Irrigation:** Young plants every 2–3 days in dry season; bearing trees irrigate at 50% field capacity after fruit set. Avoid irrigation 2–3 months before flowering to encourage flower bud differentiation.

### 2. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) & Key Insect Pests

#### A. Mango Hopper (*Idioscopus clypealis*, *I. nitidulus*)
- **Symptoms:** Nymphs/adults suck sap from flowers/tender shoots causing flower drying and fruit drop; honeydew leads to sooty mold.
- **Biological Option:** Spray *Metarhizium anisopliae* @ 1 × 10^8 cfu/mL or *Beauveria bassiana* @ 10^8 cfu/mL. Spray Azadirachtin 3000 ppm @ 2.0 mL/L or Neem Oil 0.2%.
- **Chemical Option:** Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3 mL/L or Thiamethoxam 25% WG @ 0.2 g/L or Buprofezin 25% SC @ 1.5 mL/L during flowering.
- **PHI:** Imidacloprid: 45 days; Thiamethoxam: 30 days; Buprofezin: 20 days.

#### B. Mango Mealybug (*Drosicha mangiferae*)
- **Symptoms:** Nymphs climb trunk and suck sap from panicles.
- **Mechanical Control:** Band trunks in mid-December with 20-cm wide 400-gauge Alkathene/polythene sheet at 50 cm height above ground.
- **Biological Option:** Release Australian Ladybird Beetle (*Cryptolaemus montrouzieri*) @ 10 beetles/tree.
- **Chemical Option:** Spray Dimethoate 30% EC @ 1.5 mL/L around trunk base.

#### C. Mango Fruit Fly (*Bactrocera dorsalis*)
- **Management:** Install **Methyl Eugenol Lure Traps @ 4–5 traps/acre** hung at mid-canopy height. Perform orchard sanitation by collecting and burying fallen fruits. Bait spray: Mix 100 g Jaggery + 2 mL Malathion 50% EC in 1 L water.

### 3. Disease Management Protocols

#### A. Powdery Mildew (*Oidium mangiferae*)
- **Symptoms:** White powder on leaves, flowers, panicles, and young fruits; causes premature flower drop.
- **Chemical Option:** Spray **Hexaconazole 5% EC @ 1 mL/L** or Penconazole 10% EC @ 0.5 mL/L or Wettable Sulfur 80% WP @ 3 g/L or Tebuconazole 50% + Trifloxystrobin 25% WG @ 0.75 g/L at panicle emergence.
- **PHI:** Hexaconazole: 30 days; Tebuconazole+Trifloxystrobin: 15 days.

#### B. Anthracnose (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*)
- **Symptoms:** Black spots on leaves, panicle blight, and dark sunken spots on ripe fruits.
- **Chemical Option:** Spray **Azoxystrobin 23% SC @ 1 mL/L** or Copper Oxychloride 50% WP @ 3 g/L or Carbendazim 12% + Mancozeb 63% WP @ 2 g/L.
- **PHI:** Azoxystrobin: 5 days; Copper Oxychloride: 10 days.

#### C. Mango Malformation
- **Symptoms:** Bunchy top vegetative shoots and compact malformed flower heads.
- **Management:** Prune malformed panicles along with 15–20 cm healthy stem tissue. Spray NAA @ 100–200 ppm in October.

#### D. Dieback (*Lasiodiplodia theobromae*)
- **Management:** Prune infected twigs 7–10 cm below lesion. Paint cut ends with Bordeaux Paste (1 kg CuSO4 + 1 kg Lime + 10 L water) or Copper Oxychloride paste.

### 4. Micronutrient Management & Disorders

- **Boron Deficiency / Internal Necrosis:** Water-soaked spots on lower fruit surface progressing to dark brown necrosis. Soil application of Borax @ 250 g/tree or foliar spray of **Borax 1% (10 g/L)** at pea stage.
- **Black Tip / Chimney Disease:** Caused by brick kiln sulfur fumes. Spray **Borax 1%** at pea stage and repeat after 15 days.

## Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI) & Harvest Cautions

- **Azoxystrobin 23% SC:** 5 days PHI waiting period.
- **Hexaconazole 5% EC:** 30 days PHI waiting period.
- **Imidacloprid 17.8% SL:** 45 days PHI waiting period.
- Harvest fruits at 3/4th maturity with 1–2 cm stem attached using secateurs. Pre-cool fruits to 13°C.
