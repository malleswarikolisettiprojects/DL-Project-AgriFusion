# Papaya Crop Management — India (Telangana and Andhra Pradesh)

## Scope

This source file covers **papaya (*Carica papaya* L.) crop management** for farmers and extension/RAG use in India, with Telangana and Andhra Pradesh as the intended geography. It focuses on disease, pest, nutrient, integrated, organic, chemical, harvest and pre-harvest information that was traceable to opened official sources. It does **not** make medical or nutritional-health claims about eating papaya or papain.

The recommendations below are not a substitute for the pesticide label, the current Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee (CIBRC) use direction, or advice from the district horticulture/plant-protection officer. Several university and NHB pages are older technical pages and contain products that may be restricted, withdrawn, or have changed labels. Such entries are retained only as source-traceable historical/technical guidance and are explicitly flagged for current verification.

## Verification date

**2026-09-18.** URLs and the important claims used here were opened and checked on this date. A “current” status is not inferred merely because an older page remains online; current label/registration confirmation is required before any pesticide purchase or use.

## Geography and state caveat

The National Horticulture Board (NHB) model page identifies Andhra Pradesh as a major historical papaya-producing state, but its production table is for 2001–02 and is not a current area/production estimate. The Andhra Pradesh Department of Horticulture portal confirms that the state department is responsible for integrated development of fruit, vegetable, plantation, spice and ornamental crops and provides state horticulture links. Telangana’s official Jagtial district horticulture page uses the Telugu crop name **బొప్పాయి** and describes a papaya area-expansion item under MIDH, but the page is a district programme page, not a disease/pesticide package.

The ICAR Kharif Agro-Advisory 2025 is organized by state. The papaya block opened for this file occurs in a fruit-crop section whose immediately preceding state heading is **West Bengal**, not Andhra Pradesh or Telangana. Therefore, its papaya advice is included only as a clearly labelled cross-region advisory, not as a Telangana/AP-specific prescription. No opened authoritative source supplied a complete, papaya-specific Telangana or Andhra Pradesh plant-protection package with local label status. District-level recommendations should therefore be confirmed with the relevant state/district horticulture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Baseline crop establishment and nutrition

### Site, drainage, planting and irrigation

NHB describes papaya as sensitive to strong winds and water stagnation and recommends deep, well-drained sandy loam. Its production technology gives 1.8 × 1.8 m as normal spacing, with 1.5 × 1.5 m for higher-density cultivation and 1.2 × 1.2 m for the Pusa Nanha high-density example. It gives 60 × 60 × 60 cm pits filled with topsoil, 20 kg farmyard manure, 1 kg neem cake and 1 kg bone meal. These are NHB model recommendations, not a Telangana/AP soil-test prescription.

TNAU gives a different establishment package: 1.8 m spacing, 45 × 45 × 45 cm pits, June–September as its stated planting window, and a warning to avoid planting in the rainy season. Because planting windows and pit prescriptions differ between official sources, farmers should use the state/district extension recommendation for their soil, rainfall and planting material rather than merging the numbers.

NHB says irrigation should be adjusted to soil and weather. It describes protective irrigation in year one, fortnightly irrigation in winter during year two, and irrigation every 10 days in summer during year two; basin irrigation is common, while sprinkler or drip may be used in low-rainfall areas. TNAU states that the field may be irrigated once a week and separately gives a drip/fertigation schedule. Telangana’s micro-irrigation guidance lists papaya as suitable for inline drip and requires a Telugu crop manual and irrigation/fertigation schedule from the implementing company. **Do not treat any single schedule as universally valid without checking soil, emitter discharge, weather and plant age.**

## Integrated management principles

TNAU’s papaya IPM package recommends field scouting and an agro-ecosystem analysis that considers plant health, pest and defender populations, soil, climate and farmer experience before control action. Its cultural recommendations are: frequent ploughing around trees to destroy stages of grasshoppers and mealybugs; an alkathene band around the trunk in November–December to prevent climbing mealybugs; neem seed cake at 4 kg/tree after ploughing; repeated hoeing/weeding; avoiding nearby cucurbit cultivation because cucurbits can be alternate hosts of the virus; use of the named virus-resistant varieties ‘Godakavella’, ‘Bangalore’ and ‘Coimbatore’; nursery-bed soil solarization to reduce insect/nematode damage; and good drainage.

Mechanical and sanitation measures include cutting and burning mite-affected leaves and twigs, destroying infected plant parts, covering mature-fruit plants with a gunny bag with the lower end open to collect ripe fruit and protect it from birds, avoiding over-ripening, and avoiding scratches during picking to reduce fungal infection. TNAU also lists conservation of spiders, coccinellids, *Chrysoperla*, parasitoids and other natural enemies. These measures should be prioritized before chemical intervention.

## Disease-management entries

### Damping-off (young seedlings)
- **Symptoms/agent:** NHB describes lesions at or just above soil level, a watery/shrunken stem and seedling death (*Rhizoctonia solani*).
- **Organic/biological option:** Seed treatment with *Trichoderma viride* @ 3–4 g/kg seed.
- **Chemical option:** Captan @ 3 g/kg seed (requires current label confirmation).
- **Timing and harvest caution:** Apply before sowing.

### Foot rot / root rot / stem rot
- **Symptoms/agent:** Water-soaked patches near ground level, dark brown/black rotting (*Pythium aphanidermatum*).
- **Organic/biological option:** *Trichoderma viride* 15 g/plant mixed with well-decomposed FYM around root zone. Ring irrigation to prevent stem waterlogging.
- **Chemical options:** Soil drenching with Copper Oxychloride 50% WP @ 2.5–3.0 g/L (2–3 L per plant).

### Powdery mildew (*Oidium caricae*)
- **Symptoms:** White powdery spots on leaves; leaves chlorotic/distorted.
- **Current label-supported entry:** **Thiophanate-methyl 70% WP for papaya powdery mildew at 500 g a.i./715 g formulation in 750–1000 L water/ha, with a 4–8 day waiting period (PHI)**.

### Anthracnose (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*)
- **Symptoms:** Sunken water-soaked spots on green/ripe fruits.
- **Chemical options:** Copper Oxychloride 50% WP @ 3 g/L or Carbendazim 50% WP @ 1 g/L at 15-day intervals.

### Papaya Ring Spot Virus (PRSV) & Leaf Curl
- **Symptoms:** Mottling, distortion, shoe-stringing, ring spots on fruits. Vector: Aphids / Whiteflies.
- **Management:** Rogue out infected plants immediately. Control insect vectors with Neem Oil 3% (30 mL/L) or Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3 mL/L. No chemical cure exists.

## Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI) & Harvest Cautions

- **Thiophanate-methyl 70% WP:** 4–8 days PHI waiting period.
- **Bio-pesticides (Trichoderma, Neem Oil):** 0–1 day safety margin.
- Harvest fruit at colour-break stage (when latex turns from milky to watery). Avoid skin scratches during picking.
